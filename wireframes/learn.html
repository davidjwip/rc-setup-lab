Where the car actually rolls

Not around its centre of gravity. Each end of the car pivots around its own roll centre — and where those two points sit decides which end runs out of grip first.

Built from published vehicle dynamicsNo prior maths needed
Converging links. The two construction lines cross on the centreline.Parallel links. The construction never closes, so there is no height to report.

Extend the upper and lower link on one side until the lines meet. That meeting point is the instant centre for that corner — the single pivot the wheel appears to swing around at this instant of travel. Draw a line from the bottom of the tyre through it, do the same on the other side, and the crossing point is the roll centre for that end of the car.

Two things fall out of this picture. A roll centre near the ground means the car leans more for the same cornering force. A roll centre that sits high resists roll — but it also pulls the tyre sideways as the suspension moves, which is felt as a sudden loss of grip.

The height, in one line

Roll centre height
h_rc = (z_ic / y_ic) × (t / 2)
zic instant centre height over the contact patch · yic its inboard distance · t track width

Two levers change it, and they pull in opposite directions. Raising the inner pickup lifts the instant centre, which lifts the roll centre. Widening the track lowers the roll centre for the same geometry, which is why a wider car rolls more before it takes a set.

Camber gain is the same picture

As the wheel swings around that instant centre it also tilts, and the tyre leans with it. Camber gain is how much tilt you get per millimetre of travel — the geometry quietly correcting the camber the body roll just took away.

Camber gain
dγ/dz ≈ 1 / Lswing (rad per mm) Δγ/Δφ = (t/2 × π/180) × dγ/dz
Lswing distance from contact patch to instant centre · φ body roll in degrees

Move the inner camber link up and the instant centre moves, so the roll centre and the camber gain move together. You cannot change one without the other — which is exactly why “add more camber link” is not free.
